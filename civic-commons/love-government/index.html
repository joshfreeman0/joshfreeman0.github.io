World government

Love Government

A world government founded on love.

A design by Josh Freeman: a preamble, the principles, how it is organized, the rights and duties, and a money of its own. It is written to be read, and to sit beside the towns and countries people already have. It does not speak for any real government or organization.

Preamble

Preamble

Love is already here, before any office is. A person is not a problem to be managed, and a people is not a resource to be spent. Love Government is founded on that.

We join so that no one is left without food, shelter, care, or a voice, and so that peace between peoples has a home that belongs to no single country. We keep our own towns, languages, and ways of living. We give this government only the work that is too large, or too shared, for one place to carry alone.

What we decide, we decide in the open. What we spend, we spend in Kitty Cash, a money tied to copper and published in full. What we harm, we repair. What we decide, we can begin again.

Principles

Core principles

Eight principles. A proposal that would break one of them stops, and it is rewritten before it can pass.

  • 01

    Love comes first

    Every rule is care for a real person, said so anyone can follow it. A measure that works by humiliating someone is refused.

  • 02

    Every person belongs

    Membership is a choice, open to anyone who accepts this charter. Birthplace, belief, language, body, and wealth are not gates.

  • 03

    The nearest hands

    A matter stays with the people who live it. Love Government takes up only what a household, a town, or a country cannot settle alone.

  • 04

    Care is owed

    Food, shelter, clean water, and care in sickness, in old age, and in childhood are owed to every person. They are given outright.

  • 05

    Open and plain

    Meetings, votes, and the money are published in words a member can read. A decision that cannot be read has not been made.

  • 06

    Peace, then repair

    A dispute is heard before anything is decided. The aim is to put right what broke. Force is a last answer to harm that will not stop, and the people who use it account for it afterward.

  • 07

    The living world counts

    Land, water, air, and the other animals are not stores to be used up. What is taken is restored, as far as restoration can go.

  • 08

    One honest money

    Kitty Cash is the money. Its rates are fixed in this charter and on the Kitty Cash page. No office publishes a second value for it.

Organization

How it is organized

The hearths decide. Under them sit four short duties: Keepers, Listeners, Stewards, and the Watch. A member holds only one of those duties at a time, and then goes back to their hearth.

The Hearth

Members gather in hearths, local circles small enough that people can learn one another's names. This is where speech starts, where care is first given, and where a proposal is read before it travels. Anyone who accepts the charter may join a hearth and become a member, a citizen of Love Government. Joining costs nothing. A person may leave, and the care owed to them as a person stays with them.

Children are members in the care of their hearth. They are heard on what touches them. Duties that belong to adults stay with adults.

The World Hearth

Once a year the hearths meet as the World Hearth. They set the direction of the year, and they answer the proposals that have been waiting. A hearth may send messengers. A messenger carries that hearth's words, and carries no office along with them.

The Keepers

Keepers are a small circle, drawn by lot from members who have offered to serve. They carry out what the hearths have already agreed, and they report back in plain words. A keeper serves one year. The lot is drawn again at the World Hearth.

The Listeners

When people are in a dispute, their hearth may ask Listeners to sit with them. Listeners are members who have offered for that work. They listen until a repair can be named. If no repair can be found, the matter returns to the hearth it belongs to. If it reaches past one place, it goes to the World Hearth.

The Stewards

Stewards care for Kitty Cash, for stores of food and shelter, and for help sent when a place is overwhelmed. They keep every account where members can read it. The rates of the money stay as this charter and the Kitty Cash page publish them, and the Stewards follow those rates. A steward serves one year. The World Hearth may ask a steward to stay a second year so an open account is finished in the same hands.

The Watch

The Watch is a turn of care, not an army. Members who offer serve for one season, about three months, watching for harm that is already moving: hunger, disaster, and violence between peoples. They call the hearths. The hearths decide what is done.

Decisions

How decisions get made

A decision belongs to the hearths. The Keepers, Listeners, Stewards, and the Watch carry out what has already been agreed.

  1. Any member may write a proposal. It moves forward when other members of that person's hearth are willing to stand with it.
  2. The proposal is published in plain words, with time enough for every hearth to read it, translate it, and ask questions.
  3. Among the hearths that answer, two thirds must agree.
  4. A hearth that has not answered is asked once more. After that, silence stays silence. It is not counted as a yes.
  5. If any hearth shows that the proposal would break a principle, the proposal stops until it is rewritten. A count does not overrule a principle.
  6. When two principles pull against each other, Listeners sit with the hearths that disagree, and the proposal waits.
  7. A decision is undone by the same path that made it. The charter itself is changed by that same path.

Members

How members take part

A member's part is ordinary, and it is enough.

  • Speak in your hearth, and have that speech carried, in the hearth's words, to the World Hearth.
  • Write a proposal, or stand with someone else's.
  • Offer to serve. Service is by lot, for a short, named turn, and only among people who offer.
  • Read every decision and every account. When a line is unclear, ask in writing, and receive an answer in plain words.
  • Refuse an order that would break the principles. A refusal is reported to the hearths, and the person who refused is kept safe.
  • Leave when you wish. The care owed to you as a person remains.

Charter

Rights and duties

The rights belong to every person this government touches. The duties belong to members. Joining is free, and the care remains whether a person joins, stays, or leaves.

Rights

  • To be treated as a person who is loved. Humiliation has no place here, and neither does a punishment whose purpose is shame.
  • To food, shelter, clean water, and care in sickness, in old age, and in childhood.
  • To speak, to believe or not to believe, to keep one's own language and household, and to be left in peace.
  • To read every decision, every account, and every use of Kitty Cash.
  • To refuse an order that would break these principles.
  • To leave the membership, or never to join, and to keep the care owed to every person.

Duties

  • To tell the truth as you know it, including when it costs you something.
  • To see that the person beside you has food, shelter, and safety, and to do that before you ask a wider hearth to do it.
  • To take a turn at the common work of your hearth: listening, keeping, watching, or the ordinary jobs that need doing.
  • To use Kitty Cash at the published rates, and to price it no other way.
  • To repair what you harm, including harm to the earth and to other animals.
  • To tell a child that they are already enough, and to keep adult duties with the adults.

Money

Kitty Cash

Kitty Cash is the money of Love Government. It is tied to copper, and the rates below are the rates. They are the same ones published on the Kitty Cash page.

The symbol is a cat head with two bars through it.

1 Love Kitten note = 48 carob of copper

Denomination 1. That is 3.2 Q (quadrans), about 9.09 g of copper. One carob is 500/2640 g of copper and 15 carob make 1 Q.

The count

10 meows = 1 prr. 10 prrs = 1 kitten, so 100 meows = 1 kitten. 10 kittens = 1 hiss. 10 hisses = 1 screech, so 100 kittens = 1 screech.

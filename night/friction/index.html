Friction into Fuel

Some people and situations are irritating. Instead of letting them become trouble, treat them as material. Each one shows how a market works, and where something better could be built.

The framework

  1. What happened. Describe the situation in plain, neutral words.
  2. The pattern or play behind it. What was the move, and who was it aimed at?
  3. What it reveals about the market. What demand, gap or weakness made it possible?
  4. The opportunity. What would an honest, better version look like?
  5. Night-side income angle. How could it become a pick, a sponsor fit, an event or a piece of content?

Example archetypes

These are generic types, not real people, companies or events.

  • The fake-product seller

    The play: Sells something that is not what it claims to be.

    What it reveals: People want the real thing and cannot easily tell it apart.

    The opportunity: Trust and proof are worth paying for: clear sourcing, honest photos, guarantees.

  • The hype promoter

    The play: Promises a night, a deal or a result far bigger than what shows up.

    What it reveals: Demand is high and expectations are easy to inflate.

    The opportunity: Under-promise and over-deliver. Honest reviews and real photos stand out.

  • The copycat

    The play: Copies someone else's idea, look or work.

    What it reveals: The original idea was good enough to copy.

    The opportunity: Move faster, make the original better known, and build what is hard to copy: taste and relationships.
